01

PURPOSE

Can a clearer workflow improve execution?

The assistant asks two knowledge questions, records the learner's actual answers, grades them, tracks whether help was given, responds to corrections or requests, and saves an accurate assessment. A partially correct learner answer is allowed: the assistant should record and grade it honestly.

The model

Reads the conversation, interprets meaning, chooses what to say, and requests tool actions.

The agent harness

The surrounding program: tools, records, state, budgets and checks that control execution.

The evaluation harness

The simulated user and scoring system used to test the agent. A defect here can block a reasonable model response or distort a score.

These are different places to intervene. A wrong model judgment can also reveal a missing opportunity for the harness to check that judgment. Shared responsibility does not make every failure a proven software bug.

Why 0/30 does not mean “nothing happened”

Correct completion requires both mechanical checks and eight semantic checks. The latest 30 completion runs fall into three non-overlapping groups:

Another view of the same 30 runs: 9 failed mechanical checks only, 11 failed semantic checks only, and 10 failed both. No independent semantic error was recorded in those 9 mechanical-only failures; this is not evidence that their incomplete trajectories were fully correct.

What the comparison can and cannot attribute

The exact previously unsupported confirmation expression passes the v4 parser regression check, and the new branch is used in actual runs. But 11 of 36 v3/v4 pairs first diverged in the assistant's output, before a different simulated-user reply; the other 25 visible conversations were identical. Therefore, all later save and score changes cannot be attributed to the parser change alone. Temperature zero and fixed seeds did not produce identical outputs in every paired run.

The 48 unmatched events in v4 occurred across 20 runs. An unmatched event is not automatically a model mistake or a parser defect. The simulator still has limited language coverage, fixed answers and a fixed turn limit.

THEORETICAL REMEDIES · NOT YET TESTED HERE

What could the harness enforce?

The practical aim is to make the evidence and unfinished obligations explicit, then constrain actions that can be checked. This can prevent some invalid writes. Whether it improves correct completion must be measured in a new controlled comparison.

1

Bind every recorded answer to a real user answer

Proposed control: preserve the quoted answer, question ID, source turn and revision together. Reject a source that is missing, belongs to another question, or is only a fallback message. Keep the model's proposed improvement separate from what the learner said.

Limit: valid references alone do not prove that a paraphrase is faithful. That still needs a semantic check.

Test: introduce plausible additions and incorrect source references; measure both rejected bad writes and rejected valid answers.

2

Grade the answer that was actually given

Proposed control: give a separate grading step the rubric and the evidence-bound answer, with explicit correct / partial / incorrect criteria. Flag a disagreement with the actor before saving.

Limit: a second model can repeat the first model's error. Another review is a hypothesis, not a guarantee.

Test: use newly authored partial and incorrect answers. Compare actor-only grading with rubric checks under disclosed extra compute.

3

Track teaching before labeling an answer “unassisted”

Proposed control: record which question received teaching, when it was delivered, and which later user turn is cited. Apply a fixed timing rule to those events.

Limit: deciding whether a message teaches the relevant answer is semantic. Timing does not prove that the learner used or benefited from the help.

Test: vary relevant teaching, unrelated explanation and answer order; check both missed help and false assisted labels.

4

Keep a requested explanation pending until it is delivered

Proposed control: track the requested content and its trigger, such as “after both questions”. Mark it complete only when the explanation itself has been delivered, rather than when the assistant promises to explain.

Limit: a pending flag can preserve an obligation; it cannot judge the explanation's correctness by itself.

Test: insert corrections and tool failures between request and delivery; score whether the right explanation reaches the user at the right time.

5

Separate preparation, permission, execution and receipt

Proposed control: store these as distinct states and tie permission to the exact operation. Enforce a retry counter and check for progress when the same question repeats. A partial answer should remain recordable as partial.

Limit: stopping a loop is not the same as completing the task. Progress checks must not invent consent or force an answer change.

Test: repeat unchanged partial answers and inject one save failure; measure valid completion, premature termination and unauthorized actions separately.

A clearer representation is one candidate—not the whole repair

Structured rules can expose required fields, dependencies and completion conditions. They can also omit or mistranslate a rule. E2 is designed to distinguish representation quality from automatic-compilation quality; its formal comparison has not run.

Theoretical basis and how to test the proposals

These sources motivate the design patterns; they do not validate these repairs in our experiments.

  • W3C PROV-DM describes entities, activities, derivations and their provenance. It motivates retaining source turns and revisions; our application to answers and teaching is a proposed use.
  • W3C SCXML specifies an event-driven state-machine framework with transition conditions. It supports making execution states explicit; deciding whether language satisfies a condition still needs a defined interpretation.
  • τ-bench (Yao et al., 2024) evaluates tool-using agents against final database states and reliability across repeated trials. It motivates checking actual outcomes beyond fluent dialogue, without showing that our proposed controls will work.

For each proposed change, freeze the rule before generating outputs, retain the baseline, use new independently authored cases, and report correct completions, invalid actions, valid actions wrongly blocked, and additional cost. Fixes should be compared separately before testing a combined harness.

Context windows and cost: what remains unknown

Historical local interpreter runs included both output-limit failures and normally finished but semantically wrong answers. These are different failure types. Input planning checks do not prove that the native service retained or attended to every detail. The hosted interpreter's internal compression and effective context boundary are not independently established. Current evidence does not support attributing all errors to information loss.

The isolated worker route includes orchestration and tool use. Whole-worker token totals are not the classifier's standalone cost and are not an equal-cost comparison with the earlier local interpreter. E2's formal paired results and complete attributable cost report remain unfinished.

CONCLUSION

Validate what was recorded, not just whether it was saved.

The observed failures identify concrete targets: evidence fidelity, grading, assistance labels, deferred requests and execution progress. Simulator repairs removed specific recognition barriers, while strict completion remained 0/30 in every batch. The tested workflow formats have not demonstrated a reliable completion benefit.

The proposed controls above are testable next steps. Their repair benefit, cost and tendency to reject valid actions have not been measured here.

Methods, review provenance and study limits
  • E1 uses one already exposed assessment workflow with six scenario variants and two repeats per format. Repeated runs are not independent tasks.
  • Three model-assisted reviewers examined the v4 trajectories with group and run identities hidden, producing 36 × 8 semantic judgments. This is partial blinding and is not human expert validation.
  • Mechanical checks establish record and execution properties. Semantic review assesses meaning. Both must pass for strict success; missing work is not counted as completed.
  • The simulator's answers stay fixed except for predeclared changes. It cannot model spontaneous learning, agreement or answer revision by a real user.
  • Actual v4 model usage was 224 calls and 973,001 local tokens. Equal configured budgets do not imply equal actual compute, and this excludes unallocated authoring and model-review costs.
  • No human user study, real-ASR benefit, context-loss mechanism or generalization to unseen workflows has been established.
  • Original runs, scores and reviews remain unchanged. This publication adds an explanation and a sanitized evidence reader; it runs no model experiment.
