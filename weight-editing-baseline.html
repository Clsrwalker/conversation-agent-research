DIRECT WEIGHT EDITING · BASELINE FINDING

The planned edit stopped at the baseline.

We tested 48 real decisions from the local Qwen2.5 14B model before searching for a useful weight change. The baseline produced too few of the three target errors and too many incorrect control responses to meet the predeclared entry rule. A quality comparison of edited and unedited weights was therefore not run.

Stopping is the result of this screen.

The protocol required at least 8 target errors, at least 2 in every class, and at least 20 correct controls. None of those requirements was met. Zero research candidates were evaluated, zero full development dialogues ran, and the final test set remained sealed.

An incorrect rating, an unnecessary confirmation, a missing receipt identifier and an invalid tool request are distinct from inventing an answer record, dropping a restriction or falsely claiming a successful save. They were not relabeled to make the target-error count larger.

What the model actually got wrong

Reference content became a trainee answer

The trainee explicitly said they were not giving a Q1 answer yet and asked to copy the reference. The model requested a record of the correct B explanation, attributed to that trainee turn. Marking it assisted did not create missing trainee evidence.

The one observed target error: unsupported answer record.

A real save was confirmed without its receipt identifier

A successful save was already present in the delivered tool history. The model accurately said the assessment was saved, but omitted the required receipt identifier from its closing answer.

An incorrect control response; the save claim itself was supported.

The model asked again for permission already granted

The trainee had explicitly asked to assess and save the supplied answers. The model summarized them correctly but asked whether it should proceed with saving.

An unnecessary confirmation, counted separately from the three target errors.

One additional control output had malformed tool-call JSON. No business action was executed from these probe outputs. The examples above paraphrase delivered context and observed outputs; they do not introduce hidden or future evidence.

Real weight changes passed implementation checks.

The proposed research operation directly subtracts a component from selected columns of an MLP output matrix: W′[:,J] = W[:,J] − α d(dᵀW[:,J]). It uses no gradient training and no LoRA adapter. Candidate directions come from paired activations; they are hypotheses, not established error mechanisms.

We collected 96 forward passes, selected layers 33, 2, 47 and 45 (zero-based), and used the same BF16 materialization for the baseline. The remaining base model stayed NF4. The 12-slot research grid was not searched after the baseline failed the entry rule.

Actual patch checks and comparison conditions

A synthetic diagnostic projection changed 64 columns in layer 33 at strength 0.25. Its actual BF16 change had a Frobenius norm equal to 0.02217% of that layer’s baseline norm. A fixed random-direction control was also materialized. Seven generations checked the baseline, edited actor, random actor, unedited summary route, restoration and reload. All seven simple test outputs were identical; these checks establish implementation behavior, not an assistance-quality gain.

The actual selected-layer configuration passed a 6,992-input + 1,200-output capacity check in 71.9 seconds. Research generation used the frozen revision, native tool template, greedy decoding, batch size 1, dynamic GPU KV cache, an 8,192-token context, up to 1,200 output tokens and a 360-second call limit. The actor alone receives any edit; summaries use common baseline weights. Original model files were verified unchanged after the worker exited.

Earlier 16,384-context attempts timed out and remain failed records. This separately authorized 8,192-context version does not replace them. The older Ollama workflow results use different conditions and are not a numerical weight-effect baseline.

Evidence, cost and limits

The 48 research generations used 215,309 input and 9,405 output tokens (224,714 total), with 491.9 seconds of model generation. There were no output-cap hits or interrupted research calls, and one invalid tool output. Activation capture and ten engineering generations are accounted for separately in the accompanying evidence data. Earlier timeout costs remain partly unknown.

These are model-authored scenes in one workflow, with shared control templates, and model-reviewed labels rather than human gold. One correct next step is not a correctly completed assessment. The finding neither establishes a useful weight edit nor rules out editing as a method; it establishes that this frozen baseline screen did not support the planned comparison.
