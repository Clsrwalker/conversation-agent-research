Request recognition

Objective

Test whether a more complete request-finding instruction reduces omissions without adding needs that the conversation does not support.

Procedure

24 scripted conversations × 2 models × 2 instructions × 2 repeats. Compare the usual instruction with a reminder to cover separate requests and avoid unsupported guesses.

Two independent AI reviewers scored the outputs; a third reviewed disagreements and selected examples. Each model and instruction combination has 56 request opportunities: 28 labelled needs, presented twice.

Conclusion

The coverage reminder reduced omissions for Qwen3.5 9B, with some additional unsupported requests. Qwen2.5 14B had no omissions in either condition, but unsupported requests increased from 2 to 8. The reminder did not produce a consistent improvement across models.

Scoring and data

Conversations contain 1–3 turns and at most two information needs. All content is visible in one request. Only request recognition is scored; search, factual answers and delivery are excluded. The ranges are semantic ambiguity bounds, not statistical confidence intervals.

Budget and answer updates

Objective

Test whether reserving resources for later turns and preserving existing answer content improves complete help at the end of a meeting.

Procedure

2 models × 3 setups × 4 scripted meetings × 2 repeats. All setups were rerun in the same batch with the same inputs, tools and total limits: 24 model calls and 120,000 known tokens per run.

Original setupThe model decides when to publish or update an answer.

Answer checkAdds a separate model decision about displaying or updating a candidate answer.

Budget + editing fixesReserves resources for later turns, limits repeated checks, adds partial edits and removes repeated old answer snapshots from each request.

Results

Complete help: all required conclusions correct, displayed and current at the deadline.

Answer delivery across two models

Objective

Test whether checking candidate answers for display improves delivered help, and whether the result differs between models.

Procedure

2 models × 2 setups × 4 new scripted meetings × 2 repeats. Compare the original setup with an added answer check under the same tools and resource limits. Run 16 separate request-recognition checks.

Results

Six help runs per row; 14 required conclusion checks.

Conclusion

The answer check delivered more correct conclusions, but complete help remained limited. Qwen3.5 9B answered 8/14 required conclusions while all six help runs stopped early for budget. Recorded failures included missed requests, unpublished answers and incorrect updates.

Additional findings and data

The independent recognition checks passed in 7/8 cases for Qwen2.5 14B and 5/8 for Qwen3.5 9B, with one further 9B result unclear.

The later budget-and-editing study reused these meetings and reran all controls. Its scores are reported separately. Different model families and training prevent attributing the model comparison to parameter count alone.

Study limitations

Scripted conversations, local models and AI scoring. Repeats do not add independent scenarios. No real-meeting, live-speech or human-benefit result. The repaired setup remains experimental; the saved reference version is unchanged.
