01 · 27 OCT 2026 · 24 MODEL CALLS

Can an extra review repair incorrect intentions?

Objective

Correct the person attached to a request, restore a mistakenly withdrawn request, and keep undecided requests uncertain.

Procedure

Use the same Qwen2.5 14B model and v2 LoRA on six faulty states and six paired correct states. Run ordinary tracking once, then add one review of every existing record: 12 tracking calls and 12 review calls. No retraining or retries.

Results

Target ownership and status, before and after the extra review
Starting groupAfter trackingAfter review
Six faulty states0/6 correct0/6 correct
Six initially correct states5/6 correct5/6 correct
Conclusion

The review failed acceptance. All 12 review reports were rejected, so none changed the saved state. One initially correct target had already been damaged by ordinary tracking. No repair benefit was observed, and the feature remains disabled in the default workflow.

Detailed results

The review corrected 0/2 ownership errors, 0/2 mistaken withdrawals and 0/2 uncertainty errors. Eleven reports contained invalid JSON; one had a conflicting verdict and value. The implementation passed 18/18 software checks, but no model report was accepted.

The six conversation prefixes came from five previously exposed synthetic families. The added review costs one extra call per input, so this is not an equal-compute comparison. It can change only existing ownership and status fields; it cannot recover missing records or damaged request text. Zero new review damage with zero accepted reports does not establish safe correction.

Procedure

Conclusion

Overall fit improved on all six reference answers, yet v2 favored the historical wrong token at all four inspected failure points. Better average fit did not establish better repair. The earlier 2/6 repair result and failed review acceptance remain unchanged.

Detailed results

The scores are next-token logit differences in nats at the first mismatch with the historical answer. The prompt and preceding tokens match at that point. Later full-answer scoring supplies the correct reference prefix; it does not show the model finding the repair by itself.

With v2 enabled, the top-ranked token matched the historical wrong token at all four points. With the adapter disabled, the reference token ranked first for English ownership and uncertainty; a third token ranked first in the other two cases. Positive pairwise scores therefore do not imply four successful repairs.

Three comparisons involve punctuation tokens that open or end part of an object, not a complete repair action. The fourth compares update with withdraw. The other two examples used equivalent wording or matched through the end token; an absent template newline is not an intention error.

These are six previously seen examples and four selected failures, measured once in a fixed order. Local token preferences are not whole-answer probabilities or a new success rate, and they do not identify which training choice caused the failures.

Next experiment: compare the original training-answer order with repair-first order, keeping the model, data content and training settings fixed. Training has not started.

Some captured text updates cite the original ownership evidence, so a blanket claim that the model never encountered the historical source is unsupported.
  • V2 touches and repairs 0/5 mistakenly withdrawn targets. The adapter-disabled and v1 arms each restore 2/5 target states, but those four reopen operations cite insufficient evidence. State correctness and evidence sufficiency remain distinct.
  • V1-to-v2 uncertain-intent correctness decreases from 18/24 to 14/24: 14 retained correct, four regressed, none recovered, six incorrect in both. The regressions comprise two empty patches at initial introduction, one unrelated content overwrite, and one explicit promotion to active.
  • Five injected active-status faults on uncertain intentions remain active in all three arms. The current metadata and stored history agree on the wrong status; raw dialogue and record wording say the user is undecided. A check only comparing the state with its own history would miss these contradictions.
  • The original 200 annotated patches are accepted and exactly match their original projected references while preserving annotated unrelated fields. What the tracker training data must teach

500 training updates: 200 retained old-training updates and 300 revised hard examples. Development: 50 updates. Fresh test: 200 updates from 40 families, including 80 inputs with a deliberately incorrect prior state. All splits have equal Chinese and English counts.
